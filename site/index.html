SoloScribe

Give it a recording of a guitar solo. Get back the solo written out — notation, tab and fingerings in a Guitar Pro file — and an honest, measured account of how much of it to trust.

Both samples come from the test suite: an eight-bar bebop line over a ii–V–I in F, played over a backing pad, transcribed by the pipeline with no human help. The report is the part most tools don't have — press play and compare the reconstruction against the recording yourself.

Why it exists

Automatic transcription tools exist, and on real music they all make mistakes — the honest ones say 80–95% of notes on a clear recording, and none of them tell you which notes they got wrong. You get a chart that is some-percent nonsense with no map of which percent. So the interesting problem was never "audio in, tab out"; it was the question a musician asks next: can't you check your work against the original?

That question is this tool. After transcribing, SoloScribe plays its own transcription back, lines it up against your recording, scores the agreement bar by bar, and hands you a verdict it is prepared to defend — including "medium" or "low" when that is the truth. Your cleanup time goes exactly where it is needed, with the recording in your ears.

What it does

  1. Isolates the guitar from the band (and checks the isolation didn't swallow any notes — if it did, it works from the original instead).
  2. Works out the notes, with a second listener double-checking octaves, overtones and vibrato.
  3. Finds the beat and writes real notation: swung quavers come out as straight quavers marked Swing, the way proper jazz charts do it; genuine triplets stay triplets.
  4. Chooses fingerings the way a player would — staying in position rather than leaping about the neck.
  5. Writes a .gp5 file that opens in Guitar Pro or TuxGuitar, with your chord chart printed above the bars.
  6. Audits itself and tells you what it is worth.

Measured, not promised

On synthesized test solos where every note is known in advance — a swung bebop line, a blues phrase, a syncopated funk line, each with and without a backing band — the full pipeline scores:

Bebop, swung (150 bpm)F1 0.990.970.97
Blues (92 bpm)0.980.910.91
Funk, 16ths (104 bpm)0.960.900.89

Nine of nine swing-or-straight calls correct. Full methodology, the numbers' limits, and everything it can't do: VALIDATION.md. Real recordings land below synthetic numbers — which is what the per-run report is for.

What it doesn't do

  • Slides, hammer-ons and fine articulation are yours to add — a five-minute pass in Guitar Pro, not an evening of note-hunting. Bends and vibrato are marked where heard.
  • Wes-style octave doubling needs the "Chords too" setting; in single-note mode the doubling is mistaken for overtone ringing.
  • If the guitar is buried in the mix, it does worse — and the report says so rather than pretending.

Getting your solo transcribed

The easy way: send David the recording (a clip trimmed to the solo is ideal), the key, a rough tempo, and the chord chart if you have one. Back comes the Guitar Pro file, a PDF of the score, and the audit report.

The do-it-yourself way: it runs on a Mac (Apple Silicon). Clone the repository, run bin/install.sh once, then double-click Start SoloScribe — the app opens in your browser, and everything happens on your own machine. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.

If you'd rather use something off the shelf

In fairness: Klangio Guitar2Tabs (paid) converts audio to Guitar Pro files in one step, and a free route exists — Spotify's Basic Pitch turns audio into MIDI in your browser, and TuxGuitar (free) opens MIDI and saves as .gp5. What they don't do is jazz-aware rhythm, playable fingerings, or tell you where they went wrong. Try them on your clip; comparing is half the fun.
